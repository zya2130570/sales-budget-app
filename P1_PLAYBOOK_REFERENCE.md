## 2. LTL — FAST FACTS & KEY INFO

### WHY LTL?

Most businesses won't sell a full truckload of product to their customer, or they may only have 1–2 pallets they need to ship. LTL offers the cheapest way for companies to keep up with the demand of their customer and shipping out product immediately after placing their order.

---

### LTL FAST STATS

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| Transit time? | NEVER GUARANTEED |
| Liability? | HIGH |
| Palletized? | REQUIRED 99.99% |
| Avg. Cost? | $150–$500 |
| Special Equipment? | SEE ACCESSORIALS PAGE |

---

### PROS

- More economical (cheaper)
- Can ship 1–6 pallets at a time
- More consistent / common

### CONS

- Longer transit, not guaranteed
- Pricing is very specific; customer must be exact and understand LTL rules to avoid billing issues
- High likelihood for: claims (loss or damage), billing issues (variances), delays (hub & spoke model)
- Lower GP potential

---

### NOTE ON VOLUME LTL

**Volume LTL is comparable to Partial Truckload but runs on the hub and spoke model.**

## 3. LTL — COMMON ACCESSORIALS

**LIFTGATE*** — Helps offload freight at locations that do not have docks.

**LIMITED ACCESS** — Locations with restricted access or that are only accessible by smaller vehicles.

**RESIDENTIAL** — Delivering to a home or private residence. Must book with a liftgate unless otherwise stated by customer. Delivery appointment is included under residential.

**DELIVERY APPOINTMENT** — LTL carriers will not deliver at a specific time unless the shipment is booked with an appointment.

**NOTIFY CONSIGNEE** — The receiver is notified that their freight is out for delivery.

**INSIDE DELIVERY** — Shipment is delivered inside the door of the business rather than the curb or dock. Debris removal is not included.

**HAZMAT*** — The transportation of hazardous materials that is required to bear a hazard class or hazard division label.

**OVERLENGTH (CABO WILL CALCULATE)** — A shipment that exceeds a specific length limit set by the carrier. Typically around 8 feet (96 inches).

**TRADESHOW DELIVERY** — LTL shipments going to a tradeshow must be booked with this accessorial and at the correct class to avoid a variance.

### HOW TO SET UP A SELF-BOOKER — All 25 Steps

#### PRICING (Steps 1–11)

**Step 1.** Once the customer is set up in CABO, click on the "Customers" tab on the left.

**Step 2.** From there, select the customer you are setting up.

**Step 3.** Next, click "LTL" to turn on the carriers / set margins.

**Step 4.** You will see that none of the carriers are enabled. To enable all at once, click the box on the left.

**Step 5.** Once selected, a black bar will appear and on the far right, it will say "Bulk Actions."

**Step 6.** After clicking "Bulk Actions," click "Activate All."

**Step 7.** Once all the carriers have been turned on, the margins and minimums will need to be set. Keep all of the carriers selected, click "Bulk Actions," and then click "Set LTL Margin."

**Step 8.** Set the margin that you want to be on all of the customer's shipments. A lot of reps start out with lower margins and slowly raise them over time. Please get with your supervisor to see what is best for the customer as this is a case-by-case basis. For this example, we will put 12%. Click "Save Changes."

**Step 9.** The next step is to set the LTL minimum. Again, keep all of the carriers selected and then select "LTL Uplift Min."

**Step 10.** Put the amount that you would at LEAST like to make on every shipment no matter what your margin is set at. For this example, we will put $25. Click "Save Changes."

**Step 11.** For volume margins and minimums, repeat steps 4–10 but click "Set Volume Margin" and "Set Volume Uplift Min" instead. Make sure to put at least $100 as your minimum on volume shipments as they are more expensive than LTL.

---

#### LOGINS (Steps 12–15)

**Step 12.** After turning on the LTL carriers and setting margins/minimums, the next step is to create the customer's login credentials. Under the customer's account on the left side, click "Profile," click "Preferences," and then on the far right side, click "Create a New User."

**Step 13.** Next, enter the customer's first name, last name, email address, and phone number. Then click "Create."

**Step 14.** Next, click "Set Password."

**Step 15.** Set the password for your customer. Make sure to follow the instructions in red to ensure the password meets the qualifications (your customer will be prompted to reset the password when they log in to CABO for the first time). When you are finished setting the password, on the bottom right click "Update."

---

#### ADDRESS BOOK (Step 16)

**Step 16.** The next step is to upload the addresses. This can either be done by getting an exported address book from your customer's current provider or found on the invoices. You can either download the template or manually add addresses.

---

#### COMMODITIES LIST (Step 17)

**Step 17.** The next step is to upload the commodities. This can be done by checking the previous invoices. Make sure to look in class-it to find the commodities and their exact NMFC number. Customers should never be booking a shipment without an NMFC! You can either download the template or manually add the items.

---

#### CABO PREFERENCES (Steps 18–23)

**Step 18.** The next step is to go to the LTL preferences section.

**Step 19.** Under "QUOTES," toggle on "can set freight class on quote" and "VOLUME."

**Step 20.** Under "SHIPMENTS," toggle on shipping and handling fee if applicable, default insurance opt out if applicable, and default freight insurance amount if applicable. Default freight insurance amount is what you told the customer the minimum fee would be.

**Step 21.** Under "COMMUNICATION" toggle on:
- EMAIL SHIPMENT CREATION
- EMAIL ON CREATION OF SHIPMENT WITH CARRIER THAT DOES NOT AUTO TENDER
- EMAIL ON NO RATE
- SEND DAILY SHIPMENT REPORT (IF APPLICABLE)

> **CLICK SAVE PREFERENCES ON TOP RIGHT WHEN DONE WITH EACH SECTION!!**

**Step 22.** If applicable under "Reference Codes," add any required reference codes and/or if they are required or not.

**Step 23.** Under visibility, toggle on claims visibility.

#### E-PAY PORTAL (Step 24)

**Step 24.** Email your accounting contact their CABO login information to introduce yourself and what the E-Pay Portal does for them. Encourage them to make payments online and reach out to you if they have questions regarding invoices. Copy in your Priority1 accounting rep and introduce them as your customer's main point of contact for invoices.

## 5. HOW TO BOOK A SHIPMENT (CABO)

**Step 1.** After quoting under the customer and getting the carrier and rate approved, select the carrier you will be booking with. If you quoted the customer with the margin the account is set up on, click "Select Quote" and skip step 2.

**Step 2.** If you quoted the customer a different number than what the margin is set at, hover over the price and you will see a little pencil pop up. Type in whatever price was agreed on. Then, click "Save Rate" and "Save Quote."

**Step 3.** Next, fill out the full addresses for both the shipper and receiver along with dock hours for pickup and delivery. Remember, most LTL carriers have a two-hour pickup window, so if you are trying to book a shipment for pickup today at 3:30pm, it will get rolled over to tomorrow.

- **TIP:** Save every address for the pickup and destination to your address book for a faster booking process.
- **Contact information:** Only the phone number is required; contact name and email address are optional.
- **Notes:** If the pickup or destination is closed for a certain time during lunch, make sure to include that in the notes.

**Step 4.** (Implied step — fill out commodity/freight details on the shipment form.)

**Step 5.** This is the final page you will see before booking the shipment. If everything looks correct, click the yellow button on the bottom right "Complete" and "Dispatch." If the customer has a reference # or PO, click the "+" button to add it.

**Step 6.** After dispatching the shipment, this is the final page you will be brought to showing that the shipment has dispatched. You will see a "Documents" tab that you can click on to download the BOL to send to your customer, along with the pallet labels if that's what your customer requests.

## 6. VARIANCES — WHAT THEY ARE & HOW TO PUSH THROUGH

### What is a Variance?

A variance occurs when a carrier charges more than the original rate on a shipment — for example due to a reclass, reweigh, liftgate being needed, overlength, etc. You receive a variance notification in CABO and must decide how to handle it with the customer.

---

### How to Push Through a Variance — Step by Step

**Step 1.** After coming to an agreement with your customer on the new rate of the shipment, open up the variance in CABO.

**Step 2.** Once you have selected the variance, go to the "Instruction" section.

**Step 3.** Under the instruction section, click the drop-down menu. There are a couple of different ways to push through a variance.

**Step 4.** Once you have selected the variance, go to the "Instruction" section. *(Verify the details one more time.)*

---

### The 4 Variance Options

**Option A — ABSORB (Approve with Original Rate)**
If you have made the choice to "absorb" the variance, select "Approve with Original Rate" and then click "Submit." The customer will pay what they were originally charged and the variance will come out of your gross profit.

**Option B — APPROVE WITH MARGIN**
If you added a margin percentage to the variance — for example, you added 20% margin — select "Approve with Margin," enter the margin percentage, select "Memo Instruction," select "Do Not Note," and then click "Submit."

**Option C — APPROVE WITHOUT MARGIN**
If you are pushing through a variance with no margin, select "Approve without Margin," select "Memo Instruction," select "Do Not Note," and then click "Submit."

**Option D — APPROVE WITH CUSTOMER TOTAL**
If you added a flat rate to a variance, select "Approve with Customer Total," enter the total amount, select "Memo Instruction," select "Do Not Note," and then click "Submit."

> **Example for Option D:** If a customer was charged $150 for a shipment and we received a variance from the carrier for $50, and you decide to add $50 to the variance, the customer total would be $250.

## 10. INTERNATIONAL SHIPPING

### DHL

**What is DHL?**
DHL is one of Priority1's international partners that provides outbound rates for small parcel and/or freight.

**How do I get a quote?**
Each supervisor has their own login credentials. If you need a quote, get with your supervisor and they will assist.

**What services does DHL provide?**
DHL handles outbound international air shipments and includes customs brokerage services. For larger ocean freight shipments, request a quote from Ace Forwarding or ExFreight.

**How do I book a DHL shipment?**
After booking the shipment on DHL's website (with your supervisor's help), you will book the shipment in CABO for billing purposes by going to: LTL → Shipments → New Shipment → Spot Quote.

**DHL Phone:** 1(800)225-5345

### EXFREIGHT / EXFRESSO (International Shipping)

**What is ExFreight?**
ExFreight is one of Priority1's international partners that provides inbound/outbound rates for small parcel or freight.

**How do I get a quote?**
Each office has their own login credentials. If you need a quote, get with your supervisor and they will assist.

**What services does ExFreight provide?**
ExFreight manages international inbound/outbound shipments with door-to-door, port-to-door, and door-to-port service for small parcels, air freight, and ocean containers.

**How do I book an ExFreight shipment?**

#### ExFreight Resources Available in Playbook

- Prohibited items
- Country specific requirements: https://www.exfreight.com/country-specific-requirements/
- Destinations served
- What's included in the freight services?
- Documents needed to ship internationally
- Destination terminal fees & when they apply
- How to get a quote
- How to book air and ocean shipments

#### ExFreight Service Levels Explained

**DOOR TO PORT** — Includes: pick-up from shipper, basic customs export declaration, Ocean or Air freight to PORT only. Does NOT include: destination terminal fees, customs entry, delivery, import duties and taxes, storage charges, exam fees or any other destination services.

**DOOR TO DOOR** — Includes: pick-up from the shipper, basic customs export declaration, Ocean or Air freight, destination terminal fees, basic customs entry fee, delivery to consignee address with a loading dock (within regular shipping and receiving hours). Does NOT include: destination duties and taxes, special import permit (if applicable), exam fees, storage charges (if incurred), any other destination services.

#### Destination Terminal Fees — Important Info

Destination terminal fees are fees payable to the destination terminal to offload your freight from the ocean vessel, aircraft, or ocean container.

- If you book a DOOR TO PORT service, these fees are NOT INCLUDED. The consignee will have to pay at the destination and arrange delivery from the port and customs entry.
- These fees can be quite high, specifically for LCL ocean freight — in some cases higher than the actual freight charges.
- Be wary of anyone quoting a shipment where there are no destination terminal fees included in the quote.
- If you book DOOR TO DOOR with ExFreight, destination terminal fees ARE included in the rate.
